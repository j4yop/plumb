## 2. What the jury rewarded in 2026 (the strongest signal we have)

The 2026 edition was the *Football GPT Bootcamp* (Talent Arena, MWC): 20 professional matches, Smart Tagging XML + broadcast video + ATD. Its central question, per Metrica's own blog: **“How can AI transform raw football data into tactical intelligence?”**

**The winner (team “IpurdIAr”) built a conversational system with four layers**, described in the winner's own words:
> 🎙️ **Audio context** — extract tactical signals from match commentary
> ⚽ **Ball dynamics** — infer actions directly from movement and trajectory
> 📊 **Team structure metrics** — collective behaviour, compactness, block depth
> 🤖 **A conversational agent** — route each question to the right source, return clear answers
> *"the issue is no longer access to data… the real bottleneck is turning that information into tactical answers quickly enough to support decision-making."*

Other 2026 finalists: AI tactical assistants · automated event detection from tracking · conversational interfaces · visualising collective team behaviour.

**Implication:** a 2027 project that is *another conversational assistant over match data* is a re-run of last year's winner. The 2027 winner must be the **successor**, not the same step.

**Judging rubric (proxy — not published for 2027).** The repo states that in 2026 *"originality, technical solidity, use of LLMs, football relevance and presentation quality were all weighed together"*, and that in 2027 these matter only insofar as they produce a better result. For the Final pitch we use that rubric as the proxy, plus feasibility and rules-safety.

## 5. Rules and terms — what a project may and may not do

- Labels must come from an algorithm. **No hand labels on the provided match for any purpose** (training *or* validation). **Hand labels on other matches are explicitly allowed** and must be documented.
- **No use of the private ground truth, and no human-produced data about this match from any source** (Opta, StatsBomb, Wyscout, corrected tracking, annotations).
- External data about **other** games is allowed and must be cited. Organisers confirmed this in issue #16 (IDSSE Bundesliga dataset approved, with attribution and no tuning on the provided game).
- **The competition's videos, datasets, confidential information or extracts thereof must NOT be uploaded to any third-party hosted or cloud-based LLM.** (Stated verbatim in the FAQ and OVERVIEW.) → *Any LLM layer must run locally, or receive only provably non-reversible aggregates.*
- Data must not be redistributed; the public repo may not contain the licensed match data; derived data that could reconstruct it must not be published. → *Reproducibility needs a documented download + checksum path.*
- Organisers may re-run the winning team's repository to confirm it reproduces the submitted file.
- Watching the video to understand and debug is allowed; turning observations into frame-specific rules or hand corrections is not.

## 6. Prior art map — what is saturated, what is open

**Saturated (do not pitch as novelty):**
- xT / EPV / pitch control / value models (canonical since 2017-18; a dashboard of these is a commodity).
- Formation recognition & change detection: **EFPI** (template matching + linear assignment), **SoccerCPD** (formation & role change-point detection), **shape graphs** (npj *Complexity* 2025), **MORPH** (positional heterogeneity).
- Pressing intensity: **exPressV2** (KUL, MLSA25) — physics-informed pressing-intensity metric from tracking; pressing-intensity case studies in datafield.dev.
- Restart effects: American Soccer Analysis *"Throw-in It Back"* (2026), Stone et al. (throw-in strategy metrics), Swartz et al. (causal analysis of throw-in target locations — backwards throw-ins ≈ +2 shots).
- Automatic event detection from tracking: arXiv 2202.00804, **PathCRF** (2026, ball-free event detection), MDPI *"Recognizing Events in Spatiotemporal Soccer Data"*.
- Conversational football analysts: the 2026 winner; FIFA AI Pro; multi-agent match analyzers on GitHub; *"Automated tactical analysis of football matches using LLMs"* (2026, main-model + judge-model).
- Generative tactics: **GenTac** (2026, diffusion over open-play tactics). Counterfactual passes: **Monte Carlo Pass Search** (CVPR 2026 Workshop).
- Tracking-error reliability: in-silico error propagation studies of collective-behaviour metrics (2022) and EPTS validation literature.

**Open / thin:**
- **Provenance-carrying AI for sport**: *"AI Soccer Analyst"* (arXiv 2609.11224, 2026) does verifiable evidence-grounded reporting for soccer analysts — so the *idea* has academic prior art, but no competition-grade implementation on frame-level tracking with video-clip anchoring and uncertainty.
- **Uncertainty as a product**: the reliability literature studies metric error under injected noise in papers; nobody ships a *club-facing* layer that says which conclusions survive the tracking's gaps.
- **Cross-camera recovery**: multi-camera registration is studied in CV (mastercam, modular multi-camera frameworks); cross-camera player-ID mapping is on GitHub; nobody has done *ball-track recovery across the tactical↔broadcast pair provided here*.
- **The "aspects of the game" the organisers deem most relevant** are not published — the briefs land on 26 Nov.

**What Metrica (the sponsor) already sells:** ATD generation from any video source (broadcast, scouting, tactical, drone), telestration, visual analytics, Smart Tagging, GameCloud/Nexus/PlayBase. → *Do not pitch "auto-tracking", "auto-tagging" or "auto-highlights": that is the sponsor's own product surface.*

## 7. The seven candidate projects (all assume: Phase 2 = 10 matches with tracking + event data; video/audio availability unknown — treat as a dependency to confirm)

**A — Evidence-grounded tactical intelligence ("an analyst you can audit").**
A local-first conversational analyst whose answers may only assert things a deterministic engine computed. Every claim carries: the metric definition, the frames, a video clip, the data coverage behind it, and a confidence. The system refuses when the tracking cannot support the answer. Engine = our Phase 1 possession/spell model + a library of computed tactical facts. LLM = tool-calling agent over those facts, run locally so no competition data leaves the machine.
*Why it could win:* the direct successor to 2026's winner (same genre, new depth: proof). *Risk:* a reviewer may see "another chatbot"; needs the refusal + provenance to be visible in the 5-minute demo.

**B — The reliability layer: how much of this match is actually knowable.**
Treat the automatic tracking as a measurement with error, and ship uncertainty as a first-class product: a match-level coverage map (ball visibility, player-count anomalies, duplicate tracks, identity switches), error propagation into every derived metric, and confidence bands on tactical conclusions. Ask the coach-facing question nobody can currently answer: *"which of these numbers can I trust?"*
*Why it could win:* every club's tracking is automatic and uncorrected; our own competition match has a 3.5-minute total ball blackout and a warm-up halftime. *Risk:* could read as "data-quality tooling" rather than football; the sports literature already discusses metric reliability.

**C — A + B combined: "trustworthy tactical intelligence" (auditable + uncertainty-aware).**
One system: a provenance-carrying fact engine where every fact also carries the evidence quality of the frames it was computed from, and the assistant states confidence and refuses unsupported claims. This is the strongest form of A and B.
*Why it could win:* one coherent thesis — *the bottleneck is not insight, it is trust* — instead of two half-products. *Risk:* scope; needs both a fact library and an uncertainty model to work.

**D — Restarts: the dark 40 minutes of football.**
Frame-level analysis of every restart (59 set pieces here, ~500-600 across 10 matches): the geometry of the receiving team's shape, the options available, the pressure applied, whether the second ball is won, how long possession is retained, and which target location maximises retention. Deliver a restart decision aid (throw it back or forward? where?).
*Why it could win:* a real, under-analysed, high-leverage moment; restarts are where the ground truth's dead-ball semantics bite hardest; visually spectacular (a pitch map per restart). *Risk:* prior art exists (ASA, Stone, Swartz); needs to beat it with frame-level geometry and a within-match recommendation model; ~600 restarts is a thin sample for a learned model.

**E — Pressing: detect the trigger, explain the mechanism, evaluate the counter.**
Detect when a team presses and what triggered it (compactness collapse, nearest-player closing, ball location, pass direction), attribute the press to a zone and a trigger type, measure whether it worked, and explain *why it broke* (escape lanes, protection, numerical imbalance). Uses Smart Tagging's ATTACKING/DEFENSIVE TRANSITION as a prior.
*Why it could win:* pressing is the defining tactic of modern football; coaches need mechanism, not PPDA. *Risk:* exPressV2 and the pressing literature already occupy it; must differentiate by mechanism-level explanation.

**F — Turnover geometry: the possession physics of a match.**
Turn our own Phase 1 engine into an explanation engine: for every possession loss, compute the geometric preconditions (zone of the loss, local numerical balance near the receiver, pressure geometry, escape-lane availability, receiver's body/space orientation proxies) and build a per-match causal picture — *"we lose the ball in the left half-space whenever the ball is played across our midfield with two players unpassed"*.
*Why it could win:* it is the deepest use of the asset we already built, and it answers "why" rather than "what". *Risk:* overlaps xT/EPV (saturated) and causal identification (Swartz-style) — the differentiator must be the frame-level geometry, not the statistics; statistical claims from 1 match are weak.

**G — Cross-camera ball recovery: fixing the 40% blind spot.**
Align the broadcast and tactical recordings of the same match (burned-in clock + shared event anchors + shared geometry), then fuse two independent ball tracks so the ball is recovered where either camera saw it. Turns the competition's biggest data defect into a solved engineering problem.
*Why it could win:* it attacks the exact weakness the organisers themselves call out (41% ball invisible), it is genuinely novel for this pair of feeds, and the demo (two cameras, one recovered ball) is visual. *Risk:* alignment is the hard part and could fail; broadcast homography is only 65.8% valid; the ball in the broadcast is tracked with different geometry and the feeds have different clock origins.
