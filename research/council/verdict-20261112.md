## 1. Executive verdict

**Build `ANSWERABLE` — a certified capability map for automatic football tracking.**

> *"Not another chatbot. A system that tells you which questions this match's data can actually answer, answers them with proof, and refuses the rest — with the exact frames, the coverage behind them, and what would be needed to close the gap."*

Three components, in this order of importance:

1. **RELIABILITY ENGINE** — per-frame/per-segment quality model of the automatic tracking: ball visibility, ball-out-of-pitch, player-count anomalies (>22 = duplicate tracks), slot churn, speed spikes, homography reprojection error. Emits a **coverage map** over the match and an explicit list of **unrecoverable gaps**, each classified (stoppage vs. tracking loss) by corroborating player motion and set-piece tags. Validated by **in-silico error injection** — add known noise, measure which conclusions die.
2. **VERIFIED FACT LIBRARY** — ~40 deterministic tactical facts computed from the tracking on top of our own Phase 1 possession/spell engine. Every fact is shaped as a **defensible coach-facing call**, and carries: value, evidence frames, coverage basis, confidence band, and the plain-English question it answers. Facts are **aspect-agnostic** (possession, restarts, pressing, build-up, defending, transitions, chance creation) so the library survives whatever aspect the organisers name on 26 Nov.
3. **THE INTERFACE — a router, not an author** — local intent router (classic/embedding or a small local LLM used **only to classify the question**) → fact ID → **deterministic renderer** → **deterministic refusal gate** that fires when coverage/confidence is below threshold. The model never touches a tracking row and never writes a claim. Runs entirely locally: **no competition data leaves the machine**, which also satisfies the Terms & Conditions outright.

**Fallback if the week-9 build slips:** ship `ANSWERABLE-minus-router` — the reliability engine + fact library + templated front end. Still a product, still a thesis, still demoable.

**Fallback if the 26 Nov briefs name a decision-shaped aspect (restarts, pressing triggers, build-up):** swap the fact library's emphasis to that aspect and keep the reliability engine + refusal machinery as the frame. The architecture does not change.

## 2. Why this and not the others

**The 2026 winner (team IpurdIAr) built a conversational system with four layers** — audio context, ball dynamics, team structure metrics, conversational agent — and won from 3,500+ applicants. The 2027 brief is *"pick analytical or tactical questions… analysis, modeling, interpretation or insight generation."* A 2027 entry that is *another conversational assistant over match data* is a re-run of last year's winner.

`ANSWERABLE` is the **successor, not the repeat**: same interrogative surface, **opposite epistemic contract**. 2026 asked "can you answer my question?". 2027 asks **"how do you know that answer is true?"** — and answers with coverage, evidence and refusal. The 2026 jury rewarded an assistant that *grounded* an LLM in four data layers; the 2027 jury should reward the team that **grounds the answer itself**.

It is also the only candidate built on the rarest asset (frame-level tracking + event data), needs **no public data**, **no hand labels on the provided match**, and **no cloud LLM**, and it **reuses our Phase 1 engine** — so the Phase 1 sprint is not dead work: our possession engine is fact #1, and the reliability engine is the thing that makes our possession claims defensible in the technical document.

### The three pivotal findings

**(i) The "40% blind spot" headline was inflated — and one advisor caught it.**
40.69% of graded frames lack a tracked ball, but the genuinely unrecoverable gaps are **two stoppages (~6 minutes, ~7% of the match)**; much of the rest is the ball off the pitch or in a keeper's hands, and the halftime "94% ball present" is a warm-up false positive, not a data defect. **Consequence:** do not sell "the tracker loses the ball 40% of the time". Sell the *classification*: which absences are benign, which are structural, and what each one does to the claims you want to make. Honest numbers are also more credible to a jury.

**(ii) The unit of value is a question, not a metric.**
The real product property is **which questions this automatic tracking can support at all**. That reframing is what makes one candidate coherent and leaves two half-built. It is the spine of `ANSWERABLE`.

**(iii) The real gate is the selection funnel, and nobody modelled it.**
Two gates decide whether we reach the jury: Phase 1's automatic ranking (top 20) and an **unnamed Phase 2 evaluation** that cuts 20 → 5. All five advisors scored projects against a proxy rubric for the March jury and treated Phase 2 as mere build time. Two corrections follow:
- **The horizon is 26 Nov → 2 Mar = 14 weeks, not 7.** The council's schedule panic ("two systems in seven weeks") priced a self-imposed deadline. Re-priced on 14 weeks, C's scope is comfortable and D's thin-sample problem is unchanged.
- **Score every candidate twice** — as a live pitch *and* as a standalone readable artefact (method, evaluation tables, one-command reproduction, since organisers may re-run the repo). Add **gate-legibility** as a rubric line: the project must be legible to organisers scoring on an unpublished brief, and must survive a re-run.

**Contingency we must not skip:** if the 26 Nov briefs name an aspect with a decision attached, the fact library re-points to it; the reliability engine and refusal machinery do not change.

## 3. The full project spec

### 3.1 Thesis
*Clubs do not lack insight; they lack grounds for it.* Every club's tracking is automatic and uncorrected, and no one can say which of their tracking-derived numbers survive the gaps. `ANSWERABLE` turns "we have the data" into "we know what we can claim, how strongly, and what it would take to claim more".

### 3.2 Architecture

```
raw ATD + Smart Tagging + event data + homography
        │
        ├──▶ RELIABILITY ENGINE ──▶ coverage map, anomaly list, classified gaps,
        │        (deterministic)      in-silico error-injection study
        │
        ├──▶ POSSESSION/SPELL ENGINE (our Phase 1 system)  ──▶ fact: possession spells + uncertainty
        │
        ├──▶ TACTICAL FACT LIBRARY (deterministic, ~40 facts across aspects)
        │        possession · restarts · pressing · build-up · defending · transitions · chance creation
        │
        └──▶ ROUTER (local intent only) → FACT ID → DETERMINISTIC RENDERER → REFUSAL GATE
                 (no tracking row ever reaches the model; nothing leaves the machine)
```

**Fact schema (frozen on day 1).** `id · question · value · unit · evidence_frames · coverage_basis · confidence_band · decision_phrasing · dependencies`. Every fact must be answerable *as a call*, and must declare its weakest dependency so the refusal gate can reason about it.

**Refusal gate.** If `coverage_basis` for the fact's supporting segment is below threshold, the system does not answer. It returns: what is missing, which frames are missing, which data or label would fix it, and the nearest answerable proxy. Refusal is a **feature with its own screen**, not an error message.

**Smart Tagging as a prior, not as a label.** 638 instances, 14 codes, 59 set pieces, mirror-paired attack/defence codes with opposite teams (use one to infer the other), file order grouped by code (sort by start), set-piece windows of 5 s / 35 s (windows, not instants), and every throw-in tagged `N/A` (handle explicitly).

**Event data (Phase 2) is for validation, not for labels.** Where Phase 2 provides event data, use it to *check* our facts and to measure their coverage dependence. Never to fit hand labels.

### 3.3 What the fact library must contain (per aspect, ≥1 decision-shaped fact)

| Aspect | Fact the coach can act on |
|---|---|
| Possession | which zones and phases we lose the ball in, and with what confidence |
| Restarts | throw it back or forward — retention rate of the throw-in, second-ball win rate, time to regain |
| Pressing | when the press triggers, what breaks it, where the escape lanes are |
| Build-up | line height vs the ball, whether the pivot is passable, progressive-pass rates |
| Defending | compactness, block depth, space conceded in each third |
| Transitions | possession-chain length, directness, how transitions are won |
| Chance creation | entries into the box, box occupancy, final-third progression |

### 3.4 The 5-minute live demo

1. **0:00** Coach asks: *"Where did we lose the ball most in the second half?"* → answer with the number, the frames, a confidence band, and a coverage strip under the answer.
2. **0:45** Scrub the coverage map: halftime warm-up flagged, the two stoppages flagged as **unrecoverable**, and the 138.6 s blackout shown as a gap with its duration.
3. **1:30** Ask something the data cannot support: *"Which midfielder mis-positioned before the goal?"* → **refusal**: no player identities, and the ball is untracked for minutes around the goal; here is exactly what is missing, here is what would fix it, here is the closest answerable proxy (team-level spatial shift before the goal).
4. **2:30** Cross-match capability map: the same question across the 10 Phase-2 matches — answerable in 6 of 10, and the coverage strip explains the other 4.
5. **3:30** Robustness: injected noise → which conclusions survive, which die.
6. **4:30** The meta-moment: *"Every number here was computed by deterministic code from the raw tracking. The language model only understood the question. Nothing left this machine."*
